How we estimate
  • Memory. Weights take parameters × bytes per parameter (2 for FP16, 1 for FP8, 0.5 for INT4). vLLM gets 90% of each GPU's memory; the rest covers working space.
  • GPUs. We use the fewest GPUs (1, 2, 4 or 8) that hold the weights plus one conversation of the longest length, and that the model's attention heads can be split across.
  • Users. Each conversation keeps a KV cache: 2 × KV heads × head size × layers × bytes per token. Sliding-window layers keep only their window, and linear-attention layers keep a small fixed state. Users = free memory ÷ one average conversation.
  • Speed. Each new token reads the active weights and every user's cache from memory, or does 2 × active parameters × users of maths, whichever is slower (the roofline). We assume kernels reach 50% of peak maths and 85% of bandwidth when split across GPUs.
  • Throughput. A group of users first reads all their prompts (cached parts are free), then writes the answers together.
  • Cost. GPU rent per hour ÷ tokens served per hour at your busy time. The API price is OpenRouter's cheapest paid listing for the same model, mixed by your input:output ratio.
  • Limits. Mixture-of-experts speed is approximate. Real servers lose time to scheduling and uneven traffic, so treat every number as a best case and measure with vllm bench serve.
